What a guest sees and is sent, shown on a phone.

## How to read it

Every screen is shown inside the app's own top bar and footer, at the size of a phone. Screens and
messages are numbered **section.figure** — the third screen in section 2 is **2.3** — so quote the
number when you mark a page up. The margins are left empty on purpose; write in them.

Some screens are photographs of the running app rather than pictures of a single card, because they
only appear after a guest does something, such as opening a dialog.

## About the examples

These are representative English captures of the app, made for review. They are not screenshots of a
live market, and some details are made up or held still:

- The guest is always **Sample Guest**, and phone numbers, the device ID, and the sender's number are
  made up.
- **Times are held** at ten on a Saturday morning, so a countdown reads the same every time it is made.
  The queue positions shown (7th, 25th) are examples.
- The questions a market adds to the form, and the wording of a broadcast text, are examples: staff
  write both for each market.
- The web address under the QR code is the address this document was made from, not the live site's.
- Only English is shown. The app has the same wording in other languages.

## Decisions to review

These are the choices in this document that most change what a guest does. Please look at them before
you proofread.

1. **The lottery, and how it is described.** How guests are chosen, and how that is explained: that
   registering early does not improve anyone's chances, that supplies are limited, and what a guest
   who was not selected is told. Is it clear, and does it feel fair?
2. **Arriving, and being called.** What a guest should do when they arrive without having registered,
   or when they miss being called. Today both are told to speak with a market team member. Is that
   enough?
3. **Required text-message wording is English only.** The sender's name and the STOP line are
   required on every text and are never translated, so a guest reading another language meets English
   there. Is that acceptable?
4. **A failed status check tells guests the market is closed.** See Loading and Unavailable. It needs
   a decision, not proofreading.
5. **Words that mean the same thing.** "Check in", "register", and "enter the lottery". "Text
   updates", "text messages", and "notifications". "Selected", "placed", and "on the list". "Service"
   and "the lottery". For example, the QR poster says "Scan to check in", but scanning opens the app;
   it does not check anyone in or hold a place. Which word should each one be?

Shopping for Food
Shouldn’t Start with a Race

Guests at The Bay Compassion choose their groceries, much as they would in a store. We want getting a shopping place to reflect that same dignity. This project gives guests time to register and the market more control over its process.

The Market and Its Purpose

Guests Choose Their Food. Getting a Place Had Become a Race

The market offers a shopping experience rather than a predetermined bag of groceries. Guests choose food that meets their needs. That choice is part of Compassion Food’s commitment to dignity and belonging.

By 10:31, Registration Was Full

Guests rushed the Saturday signup page; some entered only initials to finish faster. Seniors, people less comfortable with technology, and people reading in a non-native language could lose a place while taking the time they needed to register.

Fifteen Minutes to Register

The new process gives guests a full registration window before selection, with less urgency to submit. They can read and enter accurate information. Registering from home can also spare someone a trip when they aren’t selected that day.

Language Access & Accessibility

A Registration Process People Can Read and Use

Giving guests more time matters only if they can understand and use the app. Language support and accessibility were considered together, with the people who live in our region in mind.

Languages Chosen for the Community

English, Spanish, Tagalog, Chinese, Farsi, Arabic, Vietnamese, and French cover the guest journey. Language choices appear in their own languages, and Arabic and Farsi use right-to-left layouts. Guests can read registration instructions, their result, and what to do next in a familiar language.

Support for Different Ways of Using a Phone

Clearly labeled fields and prominent queue numbers make the information easier to follow. Visible focus indicators support keyboard navigation; screen readers can announce status updates. Queue number animations respect reduced-motion preferences. Those affordances help people use the process in different ways.

What the Market Could Not See

A Full Queue Doesn’t Tell the Whole Story of Need

The existing system closes registration when capacity is reached. The market knows how many places it filled, but not how many more people wanted to register.

Make More of That Demand Visible

Keeping registration open for the full window can reveal demand beyond the places available. Demographic and market questionnaires also gather aggregate information we can use in grant writing and communicating the mission.

Give the Market Room to Develop Its Policy

Controlling the app lets the team adjust lottery weights and manually promote guests as its allocation approach develops. The policy is still being defined. Software doesn’t create more shopping places, but it gives the market tools to make and revisit those decisions.

Privacy & Security

Care for the Details Behind the Counts

Understanding whom the market serves brings a responsibility to protect what people share. The app needs personal information to provide the service; privacy shapes who can access it and how it is handled.

Access Matched to the Work

Staff sign in, and the server checks permissions for each operation. Running today’s queue does not automatically grant access to historical guest records or identifying exports. Aggregate reports have a separate permission. Guests use a private credential for their own visit, while the calling board shows numbers rather than identities.

Protection Beyond the Screen

The server stores hashes of guest credentials, and API responses are marked not to be cached. Operational logs remove sensitive fields; replay settings mask inputs and marked identity details. SMS updates require explicit consent. Those safeguards work alongside deployment and monitoring choices that also need care.

My Role & Process

From Understanding the Market to Building the App

The vision came from the market team. I was the sole technical contributor, responsible for architecture, product management, requirements gathering, and implementation.

Start with the People and the Practice

I interviewed stakeholders and observed the market’s existing practices to understand how the service worked. Those conversations and observations informed the requirements and how the new app would behave.

Carry the Requirements Through the Build

My role spanned product decisions and technical delivery: turning what I learned at the market into the app’s architecture and its guest and volunteer workflows.

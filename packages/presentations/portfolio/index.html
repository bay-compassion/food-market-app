Design & engineering / Community food access

Shopping for food
shouldn’t start with a race.

Guests at The Bay Compassion choose their groceries, much as they would in a store. The market director wanted more dignity in getting a shopping place, too. This project gives guests time to register and the market more control over its process.

A purpose-built app for the market, informed by stakeholder interviews and observation.

The market and its purpose

Guests choose their food. Getting a place had become a race.

The market offers a shopping experience rather than a predetermined bag of groceries. Guests choose food that meets their needs. That choice is part of Compassion Food’s commitment to dignity and belonging.

By 10:31, registration was full

Guests rushed the Saturday signup page; some entered only initials to finish faster. Seniors, people less comfortable with technology, and people reading in a non-native language could lose a place while taking the time they needed to register.

Fifteen minutes to register

The new process gives guests a full registration window before selection, with less urgency to submit. They can read and enter accurate information. Registering from home can also spare someone a trip when they aren’t selected that day.

What the market could not see

A full queue doesn’t tell the whole story of need.

The existing system closes registration when capacity is reached. The market knows how many places it filled, but not how many more people wanted to register.

Make more of that demand visible

Keeping registration open for the full window can reveal demand beyond the places available. Demographic and market questionnaires also gather aggregate information the director wants to use in grant writing and communicating the mission.

Give the market room to develop its policy

Controlling the app lets the team adjust lottery weights and manually promote guests as its allocation approach develops. The policy is still being defined. Software doesn’t create more shopping places, but it gives the market tools to make and revisit those decisions.

These are the project’s intended benefits, not measured outcomes. Read why we are building the app ↗

My role & process

From understanding the market to building the app.

The market director originated the vision. I was the sole technical contributor, responsible for architecture, product management, requirements gathering, and implementation.

Start with the people and the practice

I interviewed stakeholders and observed the market’s existing practices to understand how the service worked. Those conversations and observations informed the requirements and how the new app would behave.

Carry the requirements through the build

My role spanned product decisions and technical delivery: turning what I learned at the market into the app’s architecture and its guest and volunteer workflows.

Inside the build

Building the process the market needs.

01

Shared rules, separate interfaces

Explicit session and visit state machines define the lifecycle. Plain TypeScript domain models work in both the browser and server, while MobX stores keep React screens in sync.

Session lifecycle ↗
02

Registering in a familiar language

English, Spanish, Tagalog, Chinese, Farsi, Arabic, Vietnamese, and French cover the guest journey. Typed dictionaries keep translation keys aligned; language selection also sets writing direction.

Translation source ↗
03

A public entrance, protected operations

Guests register without an account. Auth0 permissions protect administrative operations. Netlify Functions, Drizzle, and Postgres handle persistent state; push and SMS updates use explicit notification preferences.

04

Evidence that follows the code

Storybook exposes component states. Browser tests cover the guest journey. A printable review document combines stories with captured screens, and the guest guide runs the real guest app inside its phone.
